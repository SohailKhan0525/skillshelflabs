The argument

Every section earns its place.

A landing page should answer the questions that block action, in the order people need the answers.

01

Make the outcome obvious

Put the result and the audience in the first sentence so visitors know they are in the right place.

02

Put proof beside the claim

Place a useful number, customer voice, or case study next to the promise it supports.

03

Answer objections early

Use clear questions and direct answers to remove uncertainty before the final action.

04

Keep the next step singular

Repeat one primary action instead of splitting attention across competing choices.

Questions

What usually gets in the way?

Why only one primary action?

A single action gives the page a clear job and keeps the message aligned from the hero through the final CTA.

Can a landing page have secondary links?

Yes. Secondary links can help visitors orient themselves, but they should not compete visually with the primary conversion action.

Where should proof appear?

Put proof beside the claim it supports. A useful number or customer quote works harder near the promise than buried at the bottom.

How much copy is enough?

Enough to answer the audience's real questions. High friction offers usually need more explanation and earlier objection handling.

Should the page be indexed?

Evergreen offers should usually be indexable when search intent matches the promise. Time bound campaigns should use noindex.

What should the final CTA say?

Use a verb plus what the visitor gets, then repeat the same primary action used above the fold.
